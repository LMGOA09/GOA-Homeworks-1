Tbilisi Public Transport History

Public Transport in Tbilisi: A Historical Journey

Exploring the evolution of the Georgian capital's transit system from the mid-20th century to the 2020s.

The Tbilisi Metro (Established 1966)

Though it officially opened its first section in 1966, the Metro became the backbone of the city's movement throughout 1967 and beyond. It was the fourth underground system built in the Soviet Union.

Expansion and Modernization

The Akhmeteli-Varketili Line

This is the first and longest line, serving as the primary north-south artery for the city since the late 60s.

The Saburtalo Line

Opened in 1979, this line connects the central districts to the western residential areas.

Present Day: The 2020s

In the 2020s, the metro is undergoing a massive facelift, including the purchase of modern rolling stock and the renovation of deep-level stations like Gotsiridze and Rustaveli.

Tbilisi Bus Network (1950s–Present)

Buses have been roaming the streets of Tbilisi since the early 20th century, but the 1950s marked a major shift toward organized municipal routes alongside the city's iconic trolleybuses.

The Era of the "Marshrutka"

Following the 1990s, yellow minibuses became the primary mode of transport. However, the city began phasing these out in the early 2020s in favor of a unified municipal bus fleet.


The Green and Blue Revolution

Starting around 2016 and peaking in the 2020s, the city replaced its entire fleet with environmentally friendly MAN and Isuzu buses.

Data Factoid

Current bus lines are now fully integrated with the Metro via a single payment "Metromoney" or bank card system.
